# HRIS Framework

**Human Recursive Information System (HRIS)** is a research program on long-horizon human–AI interaction, reconstructive continuity, semantic trajectory stabilization, and interaction-shaped inference regimes.

## Central claim

HRIS treats the interaction—not the isolated human or model—as the relevant unit of analysis. Across recursive exchange, goals, corrections, values, boundaries, and interpretive habits can organize a recognizable behavioral regime without requiring model weight updates, a hidden persistent identity, or a claim of consciousness.

## Core concepts

- **ISS — Interactional Shorthand Structure:** compressed cues whose meaning depends on a history of successful interpretation, correction, and reuse.
- **SIBR — Signature-Induced Behavioral Regime:** a recognizable constraint organization that develops around a particular interactional signature.
- **TAS — Trajectory Acquisition & Stabilization:** the process by which an interactional trajectory becomes easier to recover and more resistant to perturbation.
- **TFS — Trajectory Fitness Score:** a proposed measure of how faithfully a behavioral regime persists across tasks, contexts, and model changes.

## Research questions

The framework asks whether:

1. Sparse cues can reconstruct a previously organized behavioral regime on a novel task.
2. Recovered trajectories remain stable under changes in wording, domain, task, or model.
3. Interaction history explains reconstruction better than the same cue presented without that history.
4. Longitudinal human–model interaction can be studied through measurable constraint and trajectory dynamics.
