# Start Here

**HRIS — Human Recursive Information System** is a research program on long-horizon human–AI interaction. It studies how goals, corrections, values, boundaries, and interpretive habits can organize a recognizable reasoning regime across recursive exchange.

The central proposal is simple:

> The interaction—not the isolated human or model—is the relevant unit of analysis.

HRIS does **not** require model consciousness, hidden persistent identity, weight updates, or an unobserved memory system. It asks whether interaction-shaped constraints can be recovered, measured, and tested across new tasks, contexts, and models.

## Core vocabulary

- **ISS — Interactional Shorthand Structure:** compressed cues whose meaning depends on a history of interpretation, correction, and reuse.
- **SIBR — Signature-Induced Behavioral Regime:** a recognizable constraint organization associated with a particular interactional signature.
- **TAS — Trajectory Acquisition & Stabilization:** the process by which a useful interactional trajectory becomes easier to recover and more resistant to perturbation.
- **TFS — Trajectory Fitness Score:** a proposed measure of how faithfully a regime persists across tasks, contexts, and model changes.

## Testable research questions

1. Can sparse cues reconstruct a previously organized behavioral regime on a novel task?
2. Is reconstruction stronger after the relevant interaction history than with the same cue alone?
3. Does the recovered trajectory persist under changes in wording, domain, task, or model?
4. Can recovery fidelity, stability, and perturbation resistance be measured reproducibly?
